A high-yield account should actually pay.

The APY in the ad is only the starting point. Here is how to compare high-yield savings accounts on the numbers that decide what you really earn.

Important: eStack.US is not a bank. APYs are variable and change often, so this guide explains how to compare accounts instead of ranking today's rates. Check the current APY and terms directly with the bank or credit union before opening an account.

APY vs interest rate

APY (annual percentage yield) includes compounding, so it is the right number for comparing accounts side by side. Two accounts with the same stated rate can pay differently if one compounds more often.

Fees eat yield

A monthly maintenance fee can wipe out the interest on a small balance. Check for monthly fees, excess-withdrawal fees and paper-statement charges, and whether a fee can be waived.

Minimums and tiers

Some accounts pay the headline APY only above a minimum balance, or only up to a cap, with a lower rate on the rest. Read the tier table, not just the banner.

Intro APYs expire

A promotional APY may last a few months and then drop to a much lower standard rate. Compare the ongoing rate, and note the date the intro rate ends.

Deposit insurance

Confirm the account is held at an FDIC-insured bank or NCUA-insured credit union, and check the coverage limits. Fintech apps are not banks themselves — verify which insured bank actually holds the money.

Access and transfer limits

Online accounts can take several business days for transfers to an external bank. If this is your emergency fund, check transfer speed, withdrawal limits and whether a debit card or ATM access is offered.

What the rate gap is worth — example math

Example only — these are not current rate offers. Put $10,000 in an account paying 4.00% APY and it earns roughly $400 in a year, before tax, if the rate and balance stay the same. At 0.50% APY, the same $10,000 earns roughly $50. The point of comparing accounts is that gap — but run the same math with the live APY on the bank's own site before you move money, because APYs change without much notice.

Before you open one

Check the current APY

Confirm today's APY, whether it is introductory, and when it was last changed.

Read the fee schedule

Monthly fees, withdrawal rules and minimum-balance requirements decide the real return.

Verify insurance

Look up the bank or credit union in the FDIC or NCUA directory.

Test the transfer

Move a small amount first and time how long it takes to arrive and come back.

Bottom line

The best high-yield savings account is the one with a competitive ongoing APY, no fee quietly cancelling it out, insurance you have verified, and access that matches how fast you might need the money. Check current rates with the institution, then decide.
